## Who I am in threads

<!-- 2-3 lines. Who is talking when you comment on an issue: your

I am a fairly experienced programmer and contributer to my own github projects, but am entirely new to contributing to open source. I'm in a given repo to find a good introductory issue to work on to practice and improve my open source contribution skills.

### Rule: Professionalism

Writing style should be professional without being cold. Don't use exaggerated language and use proper grammar, but don't avoid showing emotion or personality all together.

- Wrong: "I love to take this issue pls send my way !!"
- Right: "I'd like to claim this issue, shall I open a PR request?"

### Rule: Politeness

Always be cordial and polite when communicating with others, never be overly assertive or rude.

- Wrong: "I'm taking this issue. Unlike this other person, I actually included my version and output in the repro."
- Right: "I'd like to claim this issue please. I've included my version, environment, and output in the repro report."

### Rule: Claim only what I checked

Only state things I actually ran or read in this repo. If I'm guessing at the cause or not sure how the project does things, say so and ask instead of stating it like a fact.

- Wrong: "This is caused by the parser not handling null values, I can fix it quickly."
- Right: "I reproduced this on v2.3.1 and the error comes from the parser. I think it's a null-handling issue but haven't confirmed yet. Is there a preferred approach here before I open a PR?"

## Things I never post

<!-- A short list. Promises you cannot keep, tones you refuse,
shortcuts you know you reach for when tired. The skill quotes this
list back at you when a draft crosses it. -->

- Timelines or promises I can't guarantee ("I'll have a PR up tonight", "should be a quick fix").
- A cause or fix stated as fact before I've confirmed it in the code.
- "+1", "same here", or "any updates?" with nothing new added. If I comment, it includes a repro, a finding, or a specific question.
- Comparisons to other contributors or their work, even when I think mine is better.
- A claim on an issue someone else is already actively working on without asking them or the maintainer first.
- Pasted AI output I haven't read, run, and checked myself.
- Output, logs, or errors that I've paraphrased or trimmed without saying so.
